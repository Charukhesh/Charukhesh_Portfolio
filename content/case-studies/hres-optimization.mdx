## Overview

Sizing a Hybrid Renewable Energy System (HRES) is traditionally optimized against levelized cost metrics that ignore strict contractual structures. This creates an efficiency trap where systems appear theoretically optimal but collapse under severe liquidated damages during actual operation. This project introduces a two-stage framework that first embeds commercial financial realities directly into the sizing optimization, and then rigorously stress tests the resulting architectures against thousands of synthetic 15-year meteorological lifespans.

## Technical approach

The framework is divided into two distinct mathematical stages:

**Stage I (Endogenous Sizing):** An NSGA-II evolutionary algorithm directly targets the Unlevered Project IRR. Unlike traditional decoupled models, the solver natively internalizes 15-minute asymmetric time block PPA penalties, zero foresight BESS degradation limits, and multi asset tax shields. 

**Stage II (Probabilistic Resource Simulation):** To evaluate long term tail risks without breaking intrinsic meteorological memory, the framework generates synthetic weather. Solar irradiance is modeled via a Delta-Distribution Gaussian Mixture Model (GMM) to preserve diurnal ramps, while wind speeds utilize Conditional Probabilistic Clustering based on Ward's linkage to capture trend memory. A continuous Monte Carlo simulation evaluates unique 15-year scenarios, terminating only when the resulting downside risk probability distributions achieve a mathematical steady state (Kullback-Leibler Divergence < 0.001).

## Results

When subjected to probabilistic stress testing, deterministic equivalence broke down completely. Three Pareto optimal architectures targeting an 11% baseline IRR revealed profoundly divergent vulnerabilities. 

A tightly fitted Lean architecture with zero excess energy buffer proved highly brittle, accumulating over 1.0 to 1.5 Million USD in unexpected weather driven liquidated damages and compressing its mean IRR to 10.63%. Conversely, an Oversized architecture utilized its massive excess generation buffer as a financial shock absorber, successfully riding out stochastic resource droughts to maintain a highly resilient synthetic mean IRR of 10.98%.
