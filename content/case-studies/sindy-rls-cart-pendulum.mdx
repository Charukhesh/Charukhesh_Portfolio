# SINDy-RLS — Data-Driven Discovery under Feedback Control

This research project introduces a hybrid system-identification framework combining Sparse Identification of Nonlinear Dynamics (SINDy) and Recursive Least Squares (RLS). Tested on a simulated inverted pendulum-on-cart, it isolates physical plant dynamics from controller behavior in tightly coupled closed-loop systems.

## Problem

Sparse system-identification methods are highly effective on open-loop data, but they struggle with closed-loop observations. When a controller (like LQR) is active, the observed data contains entangled plant and controller dynamics. 

A conventional sparse discovery algorithm will often absorb the controller's behavior into its learned physical model. This creates the "Ghost Controller" problem: the model achieves near-zero prediction error on the controlled dataset by learning nonphysical damping effects that completely vanish when the actual controller is removed.

## System Intuition

To recover the true physics, we must decouple the controller from the plant. 

**Closed-loop observations $\rightarrow$ Plant + controller entanglement $\rightarrow$ Sparse structural discovery $\rightarrow$ Parameter decoupling $\rightarrow$ True physical model.**

By utilizing implicit SINDy on rich open-loop excitation, we first learn the pure structural forms of the inertial equations. Then, by applying RLS on closed-loop data, we can separate the hidden LQR control gains from the plant's internal friction.

## Methodology

The framework utilizes a two-stage hybrid approach:

### Stage 1: Implicit SINDy Structural Discovery
We construct a nonlinear implicit library $\Theta(x, \dot{x}, \ddot{x}, u)$ and use SINDy-PI with a two-pass Sequential Threshold Ridge Regression (STLSQ). The goal is to find sparse null-space vectors $\xi$ such that:

$$
\Theta \xi \approx 0
$$

By applying rich sinusoidal open-loop excitation, this stage successfully recovers the coupled inertial equations for both cart acceleration and pendulum angular acceleration.

### Stage 2: Closed-Loop Decoupling via RLS
With the structural form known, we reformulate the closed-loop force balance to isolate the unknown friction and controller gains:

$$
F_{\text{inertial}} = u_{\text{control}} - F_{\text{friction}}
$$

RLS is then deployed on the closed-loop data to simultaneously estimate the cart friction coefficient $b$ and the hidden LQR gains $K$, despite the fact that they act simultaneously on the system.

## Results / Findings

- The decoupled **controller gains converged to their true values within seconds**.
- The overall system-identification error remained **under 4%**.
- Crucially, the resulting decoupled physical model successfully predicted uncontrolled, large-angle chaotic pendulum motion, proving it escaped the Ghost Controller artifact.

## Engineering Considerations

A major engineering challenge in SINDy is designing the implicit library $\Theta$. If the library is too large, STLSQ becomes computationally unstable and prone to spurious terms; if too small, it misses the true dynamics. The two-pass STLSQ approach serves as an effective regularization mechanism to enforce extreme sparsity.

## Takeaway

Data-driven discovery on autonomous systems requires more than simple regression. Explicitly separating the structural discovery of inertial physics from the parameter estimation of control forces prevents algorithms from conflating software-defined policies with hardware-defined physics.
