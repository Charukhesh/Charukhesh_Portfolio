## Overview

This Python estimation research project connects adaptive Kalman filter behavior with Recursive Least Squares (RLS) forgetting factor algorithms. It introduces a physics informed Targeted Injection Kalman Filter (TI-KF) to maintain reliable state estimation during severe, unmodeled physical disturbances like an impulsive wall collision.

## Technical approach

A simulated mass-spring-damper system experiences an unmodeled impulsive wall collision, causing the true velocity to reverse instantaneously. Because position remains continuous while velocity is completely disrupted, a standard Kalman filter fails to adapt. The TI-KF explicitly monitors the innovation sequence using a chi-squared hypothesis test. Upon detecting the collision, it injects massive uncertainty specifically into the velocity state covariance, allowing the filter to briefly discard its faulty dynamic model and trust incoming measurements.

## Results

The physics informed targeted injection filter recovered substantially faster and exhibited significantly less overshoot compared to both standard and purely mathematical adaptive Kalman filters.
