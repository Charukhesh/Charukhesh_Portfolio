## Overview

Model-free imitation policies for manipulation are fast but brittle under distribution shift, while pixel-space
world models are expensive to plan through at control frequency. This project builds a planner that generates
candidate action sequences with a flow-matching policy and verifies them against a compact latent world model
(V-JEPA) instead of pixels ,  trading dense visual reconstruction for a latent goal-consistency check that is
cheap enough to run inside a receding-horizon loop.

## Problem formulation

The action generator learns a time-dependent velocity field transporting a noise prior to the action distribution
conditioned on the current latent state:

$$
\dot{a}_t = v_\theta(a_t, t \mid z_s), \qquad a_0 \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I), \quad a_1 \sim \pi(a \mid z_s)
$$

At each control step, $N=16$ candidate 7-DoF trajectories are sampled and scored against a predicted latent goal
$\hat{z}_g$ from the V-JEPA world model, and the receding-horizon plan is re-solved every $H$ steps to bound drift:

$$
a^{*}_{t:t+H} = \arg\min_{a^{(i)}_{t:t+H},\, i=1..N} \; \big\lVert f_\phi\big(z_t, a^{(i)}_{t:t+H}\big) - \hat{z}_g \big\rVert_2^2
$$

## Technical approach

**Representation.** The current observation is encoded into a latent state used both to condition the
flow-matching sampler and as the input to the V-JEPA predictor.

**Generation.** A flow-matching model samples a batch of candidate 7D action trajectories per step rather than a
single mode, preserving multi-modality that diffusion/behavior-cloning baselines tend to average away.

**Verification.** Each candidate is rolled forward through the V-JEPA latent dynamics model and scored by distance
to a predicted latent goal, replacing pixel-space reconstruction loss with a representation that is both cheaper
and less sensitive to visual nuisance variation.

**Drift control.** Because latent rollouts compound error autoregressively, the plan is executed and re-verified
over short horizons rather than open-loop for the full episode.

## Experiments

Evaluated on manipulation tasks in the Robomimic benchmark suite, comparing short-horizon receding re-planning
against longer open-loop rollouts of the same generator/verifier pair.

## Failure modes / limitations

> Latent rollout error compounds with horizon length, this is the reason short-horizon re-planning was necessary
> rather than optional, and is the main limitation of relying on a learned world model instead of ground-truth
> simulation.

## Insights

Verifying candidate plans in a learned latent space is only as reliable as the world model's own predictive
horizon, the practical lesson from this project was that receding-horizon control isn't just a performance
optimization here, it's what keeps latent-space MPC honest.

## Industrial application

| | |
|---|---|
| **Industry** | Robotics & automation, warehouse/logistics manipulation, manufacturing pick-and-place |
| **Problem** | Sample-efficient, robust manipulation policies that generalize past narrow demonstration distributions without expensive pixel-space planning at control frequency |
| **How it helps** | Latent-space verification is cheaper than pixel-space model-based planning, enabling higher-frequency re-planning and better robustness to unmodeled disturbances than open-loop imitation |
